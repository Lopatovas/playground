## You are not starting a course

You are starting a **project that will live for months**. Tutorials end; your project keeps growing. Everything you learn in Track 1 gets applied to one real codebase that you own.

CommitLoop's promise is simple: if you show up and commit real work, you will come out the other side able to build, deploy, test, and evolve a full-stack application.

## This is only as good as you make it

CommitLoop gives you **structure** — stages, assignments, checkpoints, and a loop that keeps you moving. It does **not** give you a passive path where watching or clicking is enough.

**Your outcome depends on you:**

- **Look things up.** Official docs, MDN, Prisma, Express — and yes, search engines and AI — are part of the job. We teach concepts; you fill in the gaps when something is new.
- **Think before you ask.** When you're stuck, try one concrete thing: read the error, reproduce it in isolation, change one variable. That habit is the skill.
- **Go beyond the minimum.** The project checklist is a floor, not a ceiling. Extra polish, a small feature you care about, or a deeper read on a topic you hit in the wild — that's where growth happens.
- **Own your repo.** Messy commits, dead ends, and refactors are normal. Professionals debug, research, and recover. So will you.

> CommitLoop is an accountability system, not a babysitter. The students who thrive treat every stage as practice for **self-directed engineering** — the same muscle you'll use on the job.

If that sounds uncomfortable, good — it means you're in the right place. If it sounds exciting, you're going to love Track 1.

## The loop

Every stage of every track runs the same four-step loop. Learn it once and you'll recognize it everywhere:

| Step | What happens | Why it exists |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lesson** | Short, practical theory — what a concept is and why it matters | You need just enough understanding to apply it |
| **Sandbox** | Guided, hands-on practice in isolation | Skills stick when you *do*, not when you read |
| **Quiz** | A few questions that gate the next step | Catch misunderstandings before they reach your project |
| **Project** | Apply the concept inside your evolving app | This is the point — real work in a real repo |

You cannot open the **Project** step until you pass the **Quiz** (80% or higher). That's intentional: building on a shaky mental model wastes hours later.

## GitHub is the source of truth

CommitLoop does not grade you on time spent or boxes ticked. It reads your **GitHub repository**. Your commits are the evidence that you did the work.

> The objective is never to "finish lessons." The objective is to build and evolve a real system while developing professional engineering habits.

That means one thing for you from day one: **commit early, commit often, and push your work**. We'll go deep on Git in Stage 1 — today you just need the environment working and your first commit pushed.

## What you'll set up today

- A **GitHub account** (if you don't have one)
- **Git** installed and configured on your machine
- **Node.js** and a code editor, so your project can run later
- A **dedicated repository** for your Track 1 project
- Your **first commit**, pushed to GitHub and linked in CommitLoop

By the end of Stage 0 you'll be standing inside a working codebase with GitHub connected — ready to build.
